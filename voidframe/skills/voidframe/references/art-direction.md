# Art direction: VOIDFRAME

Original aesthetic: graphic cinematic 2D / 2.5D, clean shapes, intentional silhouettes, selectively textured backgrounds, directional light, restrained palette and emotionally expressive staging. Broad reference works: Gildedguy vs Oxob (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yjOmkJHZxbk), Gildedguy & the Dragon of Mar (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1156Dq6TaHg). These are inspiration only. Reference playback was not verified during authorship; no exact shot analysis is asserted.

## Character system
Design 3/4, front, side, back and extreme-angle reference poses. Make a smooth featureless face plane with no eyes or mouth at any scale. Default omit nose and eyebrows as well. Light the form without eye-like glints, two-dot shadows or mouth-like marks. A helmet must not have eye slits. Distinguish characters using head shape, body proportions, shoulder silhouette, garment shapes and a signature accent. Use 2–4 body colors. Maintain a character ID and stable proportions across shots. Use articulated neck, spine, shoulder, elbow, wrist, hip, knee and ankle where required. Store separate limbs and occlusion layers; use IK or equivalent contact constraints. Show hands with simplified but intentional anatomy, not circles on sticks by default.

Emotion through posture:
- Grief: lowered head, curved spine, slow breath, inward shoulders, delayed recovery.
- Determination: planted feet, forward chest, clear diagonal line of action, controlled movement.
- Fear: retreat, contracted torso, protective arms, pause before motion.
- Joy: open chest, buoyant steps, broader arcs and recovery.
- Surprise: freeze, recoil, then distinct reaction pose. Never use facial expressions.

## Shape, color and composition
Use one dominant focal subject, readable silhouette and clear foreground/midground/background. Compose in values before color. Use a restrained 60/30/10 palette as a starting point, not a hard law. Choose color scripts per episode. Example night mystery: deep ink #101827, muted teal #315B68, warm amber #F2AE63, mist #B8C2C8. Example hope: plum shadow #29253C, dusty blue #627D98, amber #F3BE72, cream #F6E5CA. Not every scene should be dark or neon. Use contrast and scale to direct attention; reserve bright highlights for narrative emphasis.

Use layered painted/vector environments, occlusion, atmospheric perspective, rim light, controlled texture and selective depth cues. Shadows obey scene light direction. Keep character readable without outlining every object. Make typography editorial and minimal, only if needed.

## Motion grammar
Pose-to-pose first: preparation, action, contact, recovery. Use strong line of action, arcs, timing contrast, weight, overlap, squash/stretch within design limits, secondary motion (cloth, hair, dust, shadow), and staged holds. For running, alternate contact and passing poses with stable foot planting. For emotional acting, design micro-movement and silence; don't shake the whole camera. For rapid action, use 1–3 frame stylized smears, impact holds and directional trails selectively. Preserve visual legibility; no hazardous strobing. Use animation on twos (12 drawings/s at 24 fps) selectively with smoother camera/FX when beneficial, not mandatory for every shot.

## Cinematography
Establish geography before action. Use wide shots for scale, medium for decisions, close-up of hands, silhouette or objects for emotion (not facial details). Low angle for power, high angle for vulnerability only when motivated. Respect screen direction and eyeline implied by head orientation. Dolly/push/pan only if the narrative benefits. Use foreground occluders and parallax for depth; avoid non-stop camera movement. Match action or shapes across cuts; default to motivated cuts, not transition packs.

## Anti-slop checklist
No stick-figure proxy as final art, flat wallpaper with moving avatar, oversized gradients, glow on everything, random particles, unexplained speed ramps, unreadable captions, repetitive entrance animations, or motion that ignores mass and contact. Avoid decorative clutter. Visual complexity must serve story, not inflate render cost.
